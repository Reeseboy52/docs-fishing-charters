Captain's Journal

Sight fishing for tailing redfish in Lafitte

Captain Maurice “Doc” d’Aquin explains the skiff, the shallow-water environment, the weather, and the casting accuracy behind one of Louisiana’s most personal fishing experiences.

Lafitte, Louisiana · Barataria Bay · Fly-Fishing & Sight Fishing

What is happening in this photograph?

A redfish is feeding in water shallow enough that its tail rises above the surface. Captain Doc is standing on the elevated poling platform at the stern, quietly guiding the skiff while the angler on the bow prepares for a close-range presentation.

This is not blind casting across open water. The fish is visible. The boat position matters. The wind matters. The clarity of the water matters. Most of all, the cast must place the fly or lure where the fish can see it without being startled.

“There may be ninety-nine things that can go wrong while fly-fishing, but when the one thing goes right, you can have a great day.” — Captain Doc

The Ranger 169 Ghost poling skiff

Doc’s shallow-water boat is a 16.5-foot Ranger 169 Ghost. It is a dedicated poling skiff designed for fly-fishing and sight fishing. With one or two anglers plus the captain, the boat can work into water less than ten inches deep.

The elevated platform gives Doc a better view of fish, grass lines, cuts, and changes in water color. Instead of using the outboard motor in the final approach, he moves the boat with a push pole, reducing noise and giving the angler a controlled shot at fish in skinny water.

Why Lafitte is built for shallow-water fishing

Lafitte sits within an enormous network of brackish marsh, shallow ponds, bayous, cuts, and shorelines connected to Barataria Bay. Freshwater and saltwater influences overlap here, creating a remarkably diverse fishery.

On different parts of the system, anglers may encounter redfish, speckled trout, black drum, sheepshead, bass, perch, and other species. The number of protected waterways also gives Captain Doc options when wind or water conditions change.

Weather, wind, and water clarity

Fly-fishing is especially sensitive to weather. High wind makes casting and line control more difficult, particularly for less experienced anglers. Wind can also cloud shallow water and make fish harder to see.

That does not automatically cancel the day. Lafitte has enough waterways that Doc can often move to protected shorelines, cleaner water, or a different section of marsh. Experienced fly anglers may still perform well in stronger wind, while newer anglers benefit from calmer conditions and some casting practice before the trip.

Who should book a fly-fishing trip?

Captain Doc welcomes both experienced fly anglers and anglers who are still developing their skills. He is patient and happy to share what he knows, but a charter cannot replace the repetition needed to learn fly casting from the beginning.

For the best trip, arrive with enough experience to deliver a fly accurately in some wind. Conventional sight fishing is also available for anglers who want the visual excitement of targeting individual fish without relying entirely on a fly rod.

How the day normally unfolds

Most trips meet at Jean Lafitte Harbor around daybreak. The ride to the first fishing area generally takes fifteen to thirty minutes. A typical day fishes through late morning, returns to the marina, and finishes with the catch cleaned and prepared for the customer.

If you're hoping for a shorter trip, or want to stay out longer, just mention it when you book — Doc will work it into the schedule if conditions allow.

Equipment

Guests are welcome to bring their own rods and reels. Doc also carries quality fly rods and Shimano conventional tackle. Cut bait and artificial tackle are included in the charter price; there is currently no live-bait surcharge because live bait is not generally available in the Lafitte area.

Current Louisiana redfish limits

Louisiana's current redfish slot limit is 18–27 inches, with a daily limit of four fish per angler. Redfish outside that slot must be released, and captains and crew may not retain redfish while operating a for-hire trip. Regulations can change, so anglers should review the latest Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries rules before fishing.
